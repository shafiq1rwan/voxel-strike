## Changes needed

### 1. Wire Leech coil targeting does not match the visible coil

`src/weapons/weapons.ts:1242` considers any body-box intersection above `e.pos.y + 0.08` a coil hit. `EnemyManager.raycast` only intersects the overall enemy AABB, not the animated coil. This is insufficient for the explicit requirement for a real shootable coil region.

The review script reproduces both problems:

- During the warning, a ray through the visible copper top bar intersects its rendered geometry but misses the gameplay hitbox. That bar's center is about 0.639 above the enemy center; the body hitbox ends at 0.6.
- During folded-coil recovery, a ray through an empty opening between the copper bars misses all coil meshes but is classified as a coil hit by the current height-band rule.

Implement a hit region following the coil pivot and its solid bars. Include that region in nearest-hit selection while retaining architecture obstruction. Validate breaking a live tether with real pistol fire, a deliberate miss beside/through the coil, and aiming at the elevated top bar.

The existing focused suite manually sets `lastHitCore = true` before applying two 10-damage hits (`scripts/facility-monsters.mjs:230`). It verifies the link's damage accumulator, but bypasses the broken aiming path.

### 2. All three new rigs exceed the draw-call ceiling

The reference distinguishes box count from rendered draws: ordinary rigs target 6–10 draws, ceiling 12. The implementation creates an individual Mesh per box and does not bake static pieces by material/pivot.

| Monster | Boxes | Measured model draws | Contract ceiling |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Sentry Bud | 16 | 16 | 12 |
| Clamp Guard | 27 | 27 | 12 |
| Wire Leech | 26 | 26 | 12 |

Draws were measured by rendering one complete rig in isolation, subtracting the single floor draw. Counts are identical across idle, warning, release and recovery. Box counts pass; rendered draws fail. Preserve moving hinges and pulsing emitters while batching static parts. Add an actual draw-count assertion; the existing suite only counts Mesh objects against the box budget.

The full performance suite passes its sampled Facility, Biofoundry, Geothermal and Survival scenes, but that does not waive the per-rig contract. Its Facility sample had zero enemy rigs attached during the busy-scene measurement, so it does not establish the cost of several new monsters in view.

### 3. Visual proportions still need a focused pass

The identities, main colors and distinct moving parts are present. The comparison images show these remaining differences; these are visual judgments, separate from the measured bugs above:

| Monster | Preserved | Difference from the selected large model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sentry Bud | Fixed base, square cyan optic, paired opening lids | Base and corner supports read substantially shallower; lids are thin side panels compared with the reference's thick protective blocks. |
| Clamp Guard | Slate biped, two large C clamps, amber signals, closing/recovery poses | More upright rectangular torso and parallel legs; shoulder pistons read as small glowing faces instead of the reference's prominent amber shoulder masses. The clamping action is present, but the overall stance is less squat and braced. |
| Wire Leech | Four legs, dark chassis, copper square coil, violet emitter, folding recovery | Chassis is flatter, with lighter and less prominent knee/foot blocks than the chunky arched supports in the reference. |

Tiny scratches and fittings are illustrative under the reference contract; they are not required. Prioritize silhouette, mass distribution, leg stance and large moving parts before adding surface detail. Check the next pass in actual corridors at 320 and 240 lines as well as in isolation.

## What passed

- `npm run build` and `npm run build:poki` pass (existing bundle-size warning).
- `node scripts/facility-monsters.mjs http://localhost:5173/?seed=12345` passes all checks, without console errors.
- Sector 1 is Husk + Sentry Bud; sector 2 is Clamp Guard + Arc Sentinel; sector 3 is Brute + Wire Leech. Sampled rooms stay populated and keycard response rosters match.
- Sentry Bud remains stationary, opens its lids, fires 6-damage bolts about 3.52 seconds apart, and misses a successful sidestep after aim lock.
- Clamp Guard deals a 16-damage pinch, misses a successful flank, turns at about 63 degrees/second in the measured approach, and exposes a vulnerable recovery.
- Wire Leech links and slows the solo player, applies two 4-damage ticks over about 0.8 seconds, releases on range exit, and permits a fresh link after a level rebuild. Its direct coil-damage accumulator also works; see the aiming limitation above.

## Scope and remaining verification

This new implementation covers Sentry Bud, Clamp Guard and Wire Leech. It is not completion of the entire 20-identity pack. Spitter, Carapace, Ash Weaver and the six Freight/Excavation/Medical identities are still absent from `EnemyKind`; the other sector roster migrations remain separate work.

The mapper remaps existing placements; it does not establish the reference's isolated first-monster lessons or opener windup cap. Those encounter requirements need a placement/orchestration review, not just a roster-membership test. Existing later owner tuning (for example the easier Sentinel) should remain separate from proposed reference numbers.

`node scripts/perf.mjs http://localhost:5173/?seed=12345` fails three existing named-map scene budgets: Freight about 460, Excavation 457, Medical 466 world draws against 250. These were already flagged in the handoff; this review does not attribute them to the three new rigs.

Full campaign progression, phone readability, 30/60/120 Hz behavior, co-op support effects and the complete wall/door/height obstruction matrix were not reverified in this review. The focused suite's success is not evidence for those checks.
